## 0. Why this note is different from its neighbours

Notes `022`–`026` **derive** a classification and then ask what the practice does.
This note runs the other way: it **measures the practice** and asks whether the
measurements have structure. The subject is the owner's drawn corpus — 24 sheets —
not a formal system. That makes the evidence a different kind: it is machine
measurement of artifacts, not proof about an object.

It is worth stating up front what that can and cannot buy. It can establish that a
notation **is generated by a rule** rather than listed. It cannot establish that
the rule is **forced**. Note `017` recorded retrofitting correspondences as this
project's documented failure mode, and the whole of §4 is written against that.

## 1. The result

A zodiac glyph in this corpus is not assigned. It is **computed** from three
modular functions of the sign index `n` (1 = Aries … 12 = Pisces):

| feature | rule | values |
|---|---|---|
| **geometry** | `n mod 3` | angle `∧` Cardinal · square `⊓` Fixed · arc `∩` Mutable |
| **direction** | `n mod 2` | apex up = Fire/Air · apex down = Water/Earth |
| **baseline** | `n mod 4 ∈ {2,3}` | crossbar present = Air/Earth |

`direction` and `baseline` together are `n mod 4` — the element. So

> **sign = modality × element = (n mod 3) × (n mod 4)**

and because `gcd(3,4) = 1`, the Chinese Remainder Theorem gives `ℤ/12 ≅ ℤ/3 × ℤ/4`.

**That is why there are twelve, and why 3 modalities × 4 elements has no repeats
and no gaps.** It is not a numerological observation about the number 12; it is
the isomorphism, and the notation is drawn as its two factors.

The encoding is a **codec**: given `n` you can draw the mark, and given the mark
you can recover `n`. Invertible, with no convention table between the two.

## 2. What was measured, and how

`tools/verify_symbology_grammar.py` in the agent repo. It reads no labels. Every
feature comes off the bitmap:

| check | sheet | method | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| `base12` | Base 12 signs | locate the two horizontal rules by row density; segment the waveform; direction from which side of the strip a mark occupies, crossbar from whether the rule flanks it | **12/12** |
| `base36` | Base 36 decans | fit a twelve-tooth comb to the column-ink profile; direction from the ink split top-vs-bottom; crossbar from the **interior cavity count** (a plain triangle encloses one region, a barred one two) | **12/12** |
| `families` | Cosmic Families | colour-blob centroids on four zodiac rings, angle against `θ₀ + 30(n−1) + 15` | **24/24 within 2.7°**, same `θ₀ = 60°` in all four panels |
| `bijection` | — | pure combinatorics on the stated element-pair table | partition matches |
| `crt` | — | pure arithmetic | 12/12 cells |

The two bitmap sheets share no visual construction: one is a waveform strip, the
other a row of triangles with satellites. A fourth sheet, Base 24 cusps, was used
as a **held-out prediction** rather than a measurement — see §3.

**Negative control, and it is part of the test.** Swapping Fire and Air inside the
arithmetic makes `base12`, `base36` and `families` all fail. A checker that cannot
fail measures nothing; `tests/test_symbology_grammar.py` pins that it can.

## 3. The prediction that was made before looking

The rule was inferred from Base 12 and the Sol-Center sheet. Cusp glyphs 5–12 on
`base24_cusps` were then **derived in advance** and written down before the sheet
was examined: upper geometry, lower geometry, and the two baseline bits for each.

All twelve matched, on all four features, plus the force sequence 12/12.

And the closure is forced rather than observed: direction is `n mod 2`, so
consecutive signs always carry **opposite** direction bits — one contributes an
up-gesture, the other a down-gesture — so every adjacent pair fuses into a
**closed** figure, always. Base 24 = 12 + 12 where the second twelve is a theorem
of the first, not a second inventory.

## 4. The pair structure, and what it does NOT settle

The primer states an element-pair → mark table. It is a complete bijection: all
`C(4,2) = 6` unordered pairs, six distinct marks, each once. Grouping the pairs by
**which bit differs** reproduces the rotation classes exactly:

| Hamming distance in the element code | pairs | mark | rotation class |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1, crossbar only | Fire+Air, Water+Earth | Angle, Curve | **modulating** |
| 1, direction only | Fire+Water, Air+Earth | Wave, Line | **orientation-bearing** |
| 2, both | Fire+Earth, Air+Water | Point, Circle | **invariant** |

Rotation class is a **provable** property of the marks — a point and a circle are
unchanged under rotation, the other four are not — so one side of this table is
not an assignment at all. `CosmicFamilies` states four of the six rows
independently, and names exactly the four **opposite-direction** pairs as its
"families", leaving the two same-direction pairs as the modality operators. That
is the 4+2 split of the six marks, **generated** rather than chosen.

### The coincidence in `025` is NOT resolved by this, and must not be read as such

`MANY-TABLES-ONE-STRUCTURE` records a coincidence: `C(4,2) = 6` **tables** from
four *shape axes* numerically matches the six-primitive system's
`6 = C(4,2) via tetrahedral rectification`, and *"six TABLES is not six
PRIMITIVES; matching numbers is not matching structure."*

**That verdict stands untouched.** There are two different `C(4,2) = 6` in this
corpus, over two different four-sets:

| | the four | the six |
|---|---|---|
| `025` | shape axes — curvature, singularity, closure, junction degree | 2-D projection **tables** |
| here | **elements** — Fire, Air, Water, Earth | **marks** |

This note supplies structure for the *second*. It says nothing about whether
primitives should correspond to pairwise projections of axes, which is what `025`
asked for and still has no answer to. Treating this as that answer would be
precisely the numerical-coincidence error `025` was written to prevent — so the
disambiguation is recorded in the claim itself rather than left to a reader.

### What is still not shown

- **Not forcedness.** One designer produced every artifact. A consistent mapping
  across their own work is not evidence that no other mapping was available. What
  changed is that the mapping is now *generated* by two independent structures and
  survived a check it could have failed.
- **Not the vocabulary.** The grammar is grounded; most of the semantics it
  composes is not. Cardinal/Fixed/Mutable and Fire/Air/Water/Earth remain
  assigned. §5 is the one exception found.

## 5. One semantic that is not assigned

The Emotional Permutation Framework document states the modality set as
`M ∈ {Active, Reflective, Receptive}` — the same three-fold, applied to emotion
rather than the zodiac, with `12 = 4 colours × 3 modalities` written as a formula.
Against the geometry:

- an **angle** is a vertex: it concentrates and points outward — it **acts**;
- a **line** is a flat surface, and flatness *is* specular reflection — it
  **reflects**;
- a **curve** `∪` is concave, and concavity is the property of collecting what
  falls into it — it **receives**, **holds**, **soaks**.

Five artifacts converge on those three readings independently (Init/Act/Spark/
Active; Level/Peace/Reflective; Hold/Soak/Pass/Receptive). Likewise `∧` is glossed
**Æther** — the upper air — and `∪` **Nether**, beneath: the mark that reaches up
is named the upper one.

This meets the tier-1 standard in the grounding docs: the meaning follows from a
geometric relation that *is* the relation denoted. It is the same shape of
argument as `{'is linear' MEANS 'is linear'}` — here, `{'is concave' MEANS
'receives'}`, because collecting is what concavity does.

## 6. Two defects in the source, recorded so they are not inherited

From the same document:

1. **The intensity column is filler.** Its matrix `P` carries a third coordinate
   running 0.9, 0.6, 0.3 | 0.5, 0.8, 0.2 | 0.7, 0.4, 1.0 | 0.1, 0.6, 0.8. No rule
   generates these and one value repeats. The `(colour, modality)` part is the
   structure; the decimal is decoration presented as data.
2. **`P` is not a permutation matrix**, though the document calls it one four
   times. It is a 12×3 table of labels. The object is fine; the borrowed term is
   wrong, and that is exactly what makes a correct structural claim look unearned.

The `12 = 4 × 3` result survives both. They are presentation faults — and they are
the first thing a hostile reader would find.
