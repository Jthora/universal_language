## 1. The starting hunch (unsourced, and it held)

That the primitive **count** is arbitrary — any base-N gives N slots, and you then ask what
symmetries fit. Base 6 → octahedral, three binary axes, the Elemental Combination Algebra. A tool
and a clue, not a truth claim.

And that alternative sets — {point, circle, line, wave}, {point, circle, line, curve} — are
*different decompositions of the same thing*, with curve collapsing into wave and angle into line.

Also: that Chomsky's Universal Grammar is a **human-centric shadow** of a cross-substrate structure,
and ours would supersede his as its root cause.

## 3. Findings

**The collapses are derivable.** A wave is a periodic curve; an angle is two lines meeting — so the
primitives are **not independent**, which makes the sets **generating sets, not bases**. Vector-space
bases have invariant cardinality; generating sets do not. *That is why the count varies while the
primitives remain.*

**Evans & Levinson inverted.** Surface diversity is what universality theory *predicts* — invariants
appear only under coarse-graining. The strongest counter-evidence in the field became supporting
context, kept falsifiable by attaching the WALS coarse-graining test.

**Non-convergence on count is the predicted signature.** Montague 2, Vaiśeṣika 6–7, Aristotle 10,
NSM 65 — five traditions, five answers, and NSM's own number moved.

**Counter-evidence: easy to find.** One search each. (S7)
