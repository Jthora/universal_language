# 005 — Proof, not experiment

**Type:** correction
**Opened:** 2026-08-01
**Status:** closed
**Thread:** follows 004 · corrects a method held since 003

## 1. What was held

That the open questions needed **experiments** — "zero experiments have ever been run" was the
standing indictment, and the primitive question, the coarse-graining behaviour and the Layer 0 link
were all filed as awaiting data. The Euclid/Aristotle dependency check was even labelled an
"experiment."

## 2. What broke it

*"If our primitives are mathematical objects with geometric definitions, isn't that something we can
simply prove mathematically?"*

Correct, and the vocabulary slip covered a real error: formalizing two partial orders and testing
isomorphism is a **proof**. Searching for the actual theorems found three results sitting unclaimed:

- **Fundamental Theorem of Plane Curves** — a plane curve *is* its curvature function up to rigid
  motion. The primitives are strata of κ-space, and the collapse relations from `004` become
  **derivable** rather than observed.
- **Erlangen subgroup relation** — makes the coarse-graining survival table a *computation*. Only
  Point and Enclosure reach the topological level.
- **Jordan Curve Theorem** — Enclosure separates inside from outside; Spencer-Brown's mark *is* a
  boundary. Same object. First formal link between the framework's Layer 0 and its geometry.

## 3. What replaced it

**When the objects are mathematical, the burden is to find the theorem, not to design a study.**

Promoted to `framework/provable-geometry.md`. The empirical residue narrowed to **one** question:
whether any real system instantiates the class.

## 4. Why it survived as long as it did

Empiricism *looks* like rigour, and this project's documented disease is patching evidence to fit an
assertion. So experiment was adopted as the antidote — **to a disease proof cures better.** A
benchmark can be patched into agreement across nine rounds. A theorem cannot.

**Standing rule (F-015):** before filing a claim as awaiting experiment, check whether its objects
are mathematical.
