## A. Primary: Fernando Barba's own published method

**BARBA, Fernando; Núcleo Educacional Barbatuques. "O corpo do som: experiências do
Barbatuques." *Música na Educação Básica*, Brasília, v. 5, 2013, pp. 39–48.**
Read in full as PDF from `abemeducacaomusical.com.br/revista_musica/ed5/artigo3.pdf`.
Status: **primary, read directly.** Two pages of the sound inventory are published as images
(`Figura 1. Sons corporais A`, `Figura 2. Sons corporais B`) and are therefore NOT in the
extracted text; the inventory is known here only through the prose and through the group's
signature trio.

| # | quote | bearing |

| A2 | "Os recursos aqui apresentados são em geral acessíveis a todos" | zero entry cost, stated by the author |

**Extraction note.** Runs of this PDF came out of `pdftotext` uniformly shifted by +31 in code
point ("QBMNB" → "palma", "56." → "TUM"), a font-encoding artefact. A6 and A9 are decoded from
such runs. The shift was verified as uniform on strings whose plaintext is independently legible
elsewhere in the same document before any decoded string was quoted.

## B. The notation, quoted from the apostila at one remove

**BARBA, Fernando. *Apostila Barbatuques — Curso de Formação Básica*. 2012, p. 5**, as reproduced
and cited in *Projeto de Música São Tiago — Roteiro Oficina de Percussão Corporal*, Aula 13
(`projetosaotiago.ong.br`). Read the roteiro directly; **did not read the apostila itself**, which
I could not obtain.
Status: **primary text at one remove, with the page cited by the intermediary.**

> **Tablatura Corporal**
> "A tablatura corporal é uma forma de representar graficamente os sons da mesma maneira que a
> partitura convencional. Lê-se a tablatura corporal da esquerda para a direita. Cada quadrado
> representa 1 tempo do compasso."
> "Cada divisão horizontal representa uma parte do corpo."
> "Cada divisão vertical representa a subdivisão do tempo."
> "**A leve variação de cores na tablatura corporal representa a alternância de mão direita e
> esquerda ou pé direito e esquerdo. Os exercícios devem sempre serem feitos realizando essa
> alternância.**"

So: axis 1 = time, axis 2 = body part, and **handedness is on neither axis** — it rides as a
colour modulation of cells that already have their position, and it is **obligatory**.

The same roteiro also records that the group uses **onomatopoeia** as a second, deliberately loose
layer: "cada um pode interpretar o som e escrever um conjunto de letras que se aproxime dele."
A strict channel and a loose channel, and the method knows which is which.

## D. Biography and discography

- Death and dates: Fernando Barba, b. 30 December 1971, d. 4 February 2021, aged 49; brain tumour
  surgery in 2017 with lasting neurological effects, after which he stepped away from the group.
  Reported by Exame, Correio Braziliense and others. Surname given variously as **Barbosa** and
  **Barboza** across outlets — unresolved. Status: **press, consistent on dates, inconsistent on
  the legal surname.**
- Group formation date: the ethnography gives Auê 1993 / workshops 1995 / group emerging from
  them; press reports give "the group emerged in 1997." Status: **sources disagree; recorded as a
  range, 1995–1997, not an integer.**
- Signature trio: Barba "divides his art into chest, snap and clap," and with these "anyone can
  make music without a conventional instrument" — ISTOÉ profile. `peito-estalo-palma` is reported
  as the group's trademark. Status: **press, read at second hand via search summary.**
- "Você Chegou" = the Portuguese version of "Beautiful Creatures" from **Rio 2 (2014)**, the Blue
  Macaw tribe's anthem welcoming Jade; performed by Barbatuques with Andy Garcia and Rita Moreno;
  on the *Rio 2* soundtrack and as a bonus track on ***Ayú*** (2015). Credited to **André Hosoi**
  (Cifra Club: "Andre Hosoi and Barbatuques"; another listing adds Renato Epstein). Status:
  **catalogue/credit listings; the two listings disagree on the second writer.**
  **Lyrics not reproduced here.**
- *Ayú* (2015) carries originals by members plus themes by **Naná Vasconcelos** and **Hermeto
  Pascoal**, who declined the "wizard" label and preferred "apprentice of universal music."
  Status: **press.**
- "Baianá": roots in northeastern popular tradition, with an authored version attributed to
  **Maria do Carmo Barbosa**; Barbatuques' contribution is the body-percussion arrangement on
  *O Seguinte É Esse* (2005), not the melody or lyrics. Status: **sources disagree on whether the
  underlying song is traditional or authored; unresolved.**

## E. The claim they actually make about universality

No source found uses "linguagem universal." What is attested: a member saying "música corporal faz
parte da humanidade"; coverage describing a "linguagem de percussão e música corporal"; and A2/C1,
that the resources are accessible to all. That is **universal availability of a medium**, not a
universal language. Status: **absence of a phrase after searching is weak evidence; recorded as
"not found," not as "they never said it."**

## F. Motor learning: orientation reversal is not a parameter

**TELGEN, Sebastian; PARVIN, Darius; DIEDRICHSEN, Jörn. "Mirror Reversal and Visual Rotation Are
Learned and Consolidated via Separate Mechanisms: Recalibrating or Learning De Novo?" *Journal of
Neuroscience* 34(41): 13768, 2014.**
Status: **abstract and secondary listings only — the full text returned HTTP 403 and I did not
read it.** Sample sizes and statistics are therefore unverified here.

Reported findings: under mirror reversal, correct feedforward commands and feedback corrections
appeared only at longer latencies and the earliest responses still went in the non-mirrored
direction after two sessions; under visual rotation, fast feedback responses adapted immediately.
Across sessions, rotation memory **decayed** while mirror reversal showed **offline gains**. The
authors conclude that the motor system **acquires a new control policy** for mirror reversal and
**recalibrates an existing one** for rotation.

A 2024 *Scientific Reports* study is reported to find reach aftereffects only after rotation
training, with mirror reversal showing slower initiation and greater inter-participant variability.
Status: **search summary only, not read.**

## G. What I looked for and did NOT find

- **Retrograde rhythm recognition.** No study found that tests whether a rhythm played backwards
  is recognised as the same rhythm. Adjacent and real: serial-order asymmetry in complexity and
  arousal ratings of syncopated themes; greater sensitivity to timing differences when a high tone
  follows a low one than the reverse; and Bharucha & Pryor (1986), "Disrupting the isochrony
  underlying rhythm: An asymmetry in discrimination," which surfaced **as a bibliographic entry
  only**. Status: **the strong claim is unestablished.**
- **Dance pedagogy on mirrored instruction.** Found: mirrored 2D demonstrations require learners
  to mentally reverse left and right, linked to higher cognitive load and slower motor learning
  (an arXiv AR-training paper); and a ballet study reporting up to 26% more repetitions to the
  right than the left (Farrar-Baker & Wilmerding, 2006). Not found: a study directly testing
  instructor-facing mirrored teaching in dance. Status: **supportive but indirect.**
- **Peer-reviewed support for the gumboot coded-signal claim.** See §7 of the note. Not found.
